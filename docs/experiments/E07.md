# E07: what does the instrument cost the owner per day?

## Why this matters (30 seconds)

The kickoff gives the instrument ten minutes of the owner's day. Past that, forecasts
become a reflex (G17) and the study measures endurance instead of understanding. V9 in
`docs/preregistration.md` 1.7 makes the daily median of task and probe time a gate:
above ten minutes, the design changes before Stage 2. The defaults r, k_e, k_r and k_p
(design 6.2) were chosen without a cost measurement. This experiment is that measurement.

## Decision rule, written before the run (2026-10-19)

Per UTC day with any task or probe row in the ledger:

- task seconds: a treatment task's last forecast timestamp minus its registration
  timestamp. The forecast rows are written once, after the last answer, so this is the
  time from command start to the last answer. A control task stores no end, and neither
  does a treatment task with no forecast row, so their seconds are unknown and that day's
  total is a lower bound.
- probe seconds: the sum of `probe_responses.seconds`, each the time from a question to
  its accepted answer.

V9's time limb passes iff the median over days is at most 600 s, with every lower-bound
day counted as above 600 s. An unknown never counts toward a pass.

Also reported: seconds per task and per probe item (design Q2), forecast and probe skip
shares, treatment tasks with no forecast row, `abandoned` flags, and the row counts of
drift, decision, knockout and Pathfinder administrations, whose seconds the ledger does
not hold.

## What we did

`experiments/E07/cost.py` reads `tasks`, `forecasts`, `probe_responses` and `flags` from a
ledger through `store.Store`, read only. It cannot run on the owner's data until the
pilot exists. Run against this machine's `$PRAXIS_HOME` it prints "no ledger" and stops.
It never creates a ledger.

To check the runner itself, `--selfcheck` plants three days through store.py into a
temporary `$PRAXIS_HOME`, with the answers worked by hand before the run:

| Day | Planted | Expected total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | treatment task, forecasts written 90 s after registration, 5 components with 1 left blank; probes of 20 s, 40 s and a 5 s skip | 155 s, exact |
| 2 | treatment task, forecasts after 180 s; a control task; one 100 s probe | 280 s, lower bound (control) |
| 3 | treatment task with no forecast row; one 30 s probe | 30 s, lower bound (no end) |

Expected: task seconds [90, 180]; median 155 s; worst-case verdict fail, because two of
three days are lower bounds; forecast skip share 0.1 (1 of 10); probe skip share 0.2 (1 of
5); one treatment task without forecasts.

## What we measured

- Self-check: every expected value matched. **PASS.**
- Break-the-fix: with lower-bound days scored as their known part (the `math.inf`
  replaced by the day's sum), the self-check fails on `v9_time_pass` (True against
  False). The rule's conservative reading is the only thing that catches an unknown
  control-task cost. Restored, PASS.
- Owner data: none. No pilot row exists on any machine yet.

## What it means

The runner is ready for the pilot. Two of the costs it is asked for cannot be measured
from the current ledger:

- Control-task registration time. `praxis task` in the control arm writes the task row
  with the start time and nothing after it. While control tasks exist, every day holding
  one is a lower bound, and the worst-case V9 reading fails it. A `tasks` end timestamp,
  or a per-task seconds column written by task.py, would close this. That is a schema
  change for stage A or C, not an experiment change.
- Drift, decision, knockout and Pathfinder administration time. Their tables hold one
  timestamp per row and no duration, so the runner counts their rows and reports their
  seconds as unknown. C1's cost (design 4.8, "cost needs measuring") is unmeasurable
  until `pathfinder_runs` carries seconds.

## What we rejected

- Using the gap between consecutive probe responses as probe time. Answers can be hours
  apart within a day. Each response already stores its own seconds.
- Treating a lower-bound day as its known part. That turns "unknown" into "cheap", which
  is the zero-fill invariant 4 forbids.

## How sure we are

- The arithmetic is checked on planted rows, not on the owner's behaviour. Every cost
  figure for the owner needs measuring in the pilot.
- Task seconds assume the CLI passes `praxis task` the clock at command start as its
  `now`. task.py writes the forecast rows with its own clock after the last answer. If
  stage C passes another time, the figure shifts by the difference.
